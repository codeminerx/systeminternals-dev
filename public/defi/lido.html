1 What is Lido?

Ethereum's transition to Proof-of-Stake in September 2022 fundamentally changed how the network secures itself. Instead of miners expending massive amounts of energy, validators — operators who stake 32 ETH — are responsible for proposing and attesting to blocks. These validators earn rewards for their service, but there's a catch: to become a validator, you need to lock up 32 ETH (currently worth ~$80,000+). And once staked, your ETH is not immediately accessible — it's locked until withdrawals are enabled, and even then there's a queue.

Lido solves this friction. It's a decentralized liquid staking protocol that lets anyone stake any amount of ETH — even fractions of one — and receive stETH in return. stETH is a token that represents your deposited ETH plus all accumulated staking rewards. Because it's a token, you can use it, trade it, or deploy it in DeFi while your ETH continues to earn rewards on the Beacon Chain.

Here's the key difference: when you stake directly through Ethereum, your ETH is locked and unusable until you exit the validator queue (which can take weeks or months during high demand). But when you stake via Lido, you receive stETH immediately. That stETH is redeemable 1:1 for ETH (subject to the withdrawal queue), but in the meantime, you can use it as collateral in Aave, provide liquidity on Curve, or integrate it into countless other DeFi protocols. You're earning staking rewards AND maintaining liquidity — the best of both worlds.

11 Glossary
stETH
Lido's staking receipt token. 1 stETH = 1 ETH of staked assets + accumulated rewards. Balance updates daily via rebase.
Validator
A node that has deposited 32 ETH and participates in Ethereum's Proof-of-Stake consensus — proposing and attesting blocks.
Beacon Chain
Ethereum's consensus layer that coordinates the validator set, handles random beacon generation, and manages the exit queue.
Withdrawal Queue
The pending list of validators waiting to exit and receive their ETH back. Limited to ~900 exits per day.
Liquid Staking
A staking mechanism that issues a liquid token (like stETH) representing the staked assets, so users retain composability and liquidity.
Node Operator
A professional infrastructure provider approved by Lido DAO to run validators on behalf of the protocol.
MEV Boost
A middleware that allows validators to capture maximal extractable value from block ordering, boosting staking rewards for stETH holders.
Lido DAO
The decentralized autonomous organization governing Lido — LDO token holders vote on protocol upgrades, node operators, and treasury decisions.
Rebase
A mechanism where stETH total supply increases proportionally so each holder's balance grows automatically without manual claiming.
Slashing
A penalty that destroys part of a validator's stake for malicious behavior like double-signing or causing chain reorganization.
12 Risks to Understand Before Staking
⚡

Slashing Risk

If a Lido validator double-signs or behaves maliciously, a portion of the staked ETH is destroyed. Lido has insurance funds, but losses could theoretically fall on stETH holders.

Medium-High
🔮

Oracle Risk

Lido relies on oracles to report validator performance and distribute rewards. A compromised oracle could manipulate reporting data.

Medium-High
🏛️

Centralization Risk

Lido holds ~30% of all staked ETH. Critics argue this gives Lido disproportionate influence over Ethereum's validator set and governance.

Medium
💱

stETH Depeg Risk

Under extreme conditions (mass unstaking, crisis of confidence), stETH could trade below 1 ETH. The stETH/ETH Curve pool has seen divergences during high-stress periods.

Medium
📜

Smart Contract Risk

Lido's contracts have been audited by multiple firms and have a track record, but bugs or exploits could theoretically drain funds. Lido has a $150M insurance fund.

Low-Medium
🚪

Withdrawal Queue Risk

In extreme demand scenarios, the exit queue could stretch to weeks or months, limiting your ability to quickly exit and reclaim ETH. This is a protocol-level constraint, not a Lido issue.

Low
